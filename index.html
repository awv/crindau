TOPOGRAPHICAL CONTEXT

From Tudor Farmlands to River Furnaces

Crindau occupies a low-lying riverside corridor on the western bank of the Usk, immediately north of Newport. For centuries an agrarian pocket held by the stout stone gables of Crindau House (c.1580), the district became a powerhouse of Victorian utility, glassmaking, and sweet manufacturing.

Between 1870 and the First World War, tidal wharves and Monmouthshire rail sidings attracted chemical works, iron foundries, and the colossal gasworks, bordered by an intricate grid of brick terraces built for the artisan workforce.

LEAD INDUSTRIAL STUDY

The Confectionery Empire: Lovell’s & Rexville

A commercial empire that employed generations of Crindau residents and established a famous sporting institution.

Manufacturing • Confectionery Active 1913 – 2005

G.F. Lovell & Co. Ltd • Rexville Works

Established as a wholesale confectionery venture by George Frederick Lovell, the works expanded rapidly along the southern frontage of Albany Street and the railway sidings. Famous for products such as Milky Lunch, toffees, and boiled sweets, the factory became one of Newport's largest private employers.

Beyond confectionery, the firm fostered a renowned community culture centred on the Rexville Athletic Ground. Lovells Athletic F.C. drew thousands of supporters for Southern League clashes and memorable Welsh Cup campaigns.

Primary Products Toffees, Milky Lunch, Chocolates
Key Personnel G.F. Lovell (Founder)
Social Fabric Rexville Ground, Lovells Athletic FC
